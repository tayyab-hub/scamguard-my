# Delivery training

The official rubric assesses clear speech, audibility to the whole audience and eye contact. The plan below prepares those behaviours; it is not evidence that they have occurred. No live Delivery mark is awarded from a script. Timing and audience-focus targets are practice aids, not institutional thresholds.

## Voice and presence

Aim for approximately 120–140 spoken words per minute during explanation, with slower delivery for a denominator, limitation or technical diagram. Pause after the opening problem, after the words “not a fraud probability”, and before interpreting the confusion matrix. Emphasise **evidence**, **owner**, **separate confidence**, and **not yet measured** where they change the meaning. Speak toward a listener at the back of the room and ask that person to rate audibility during rehearsal. Do not solve audibility by rushing or shouting.

Record a practice run. Note repeated “um”, “basically”, “actually” and “you know”; replace the habitual filler with a short silent pause. Use a stable stance, visible hands and purposeful gestures toward the diagram under discussion. Avoid pacing, rocking, clicking a pen or hiding behind the laptop. Keep a diagram explanation conversational: point to one request path at a time.

Target 70–80% audience focus where practical. Move attention between different parts of the room, finishing a thought before shifting. Glance at a slide to locate the next element, then explain it to the audience. Do not read the on-slide text or attempt eye contact while an essential click requires attention.

## Demonstration and questions

Explain the next action before clicking. After a result appears, look up and state what it means before pointing out one item of evidence and one limitation. For example: “The number matches a numbering pattern, but identity remains unknown.” Do not silently read the page. If a request stalls, state the failure, avoid duplicate submission, and use the prepared fallback after the allotted wait.

For questions use **direct answer → short reasoning → evidence/example**. Start with “No” when the answer is no. Do not recite a long architecture explanation when asked for a model denominator. If unsure, say exactly what is missing: “That device was not independently recorded in my test evidence.” Then describe the relevant test boundary. Never bluff a DOI, metric, configuration value or experience.

## Four rehearsals

| Rehearsal | Activity | Observable pass condition | Record to retain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Technical accuracy | Explain each slide and answer ten randomly chosen technical questions with code/report open afterwards. | Correct models, class mappings, denominators, ownership and QR/privacy boundaries; every correction recorded. | Date, questions selected, inaccurate statements and corrected evidence paths. |
| 2 Timing | Deliver the complete plan including the 150-second demo; use a timer and one observer. | Total inside 10–15 minutes; each overrun identified; no invented result substituted for a failed demo. | Slide start/end times, demo delays and actual total. |
| 3 No-notes delivery | Use only slides and a small cue card; ask an observer at the back to check voice and eye contact. | Audible throughout; explanations in own words; approximately 70–80% audience focus where practical. | Observer comments, approximate audience-focus sample and filler counts. |
| 4 Hostile examiner Q&A | Ask a peer to challenge data bias, model accuracy, IDOR, CSRF, CRC and missing human evidence. | Direct bounded answers, no bluffing, evidence opened on request; limitations acknowledged. | Questions, answer quality, unresolved topics and follow-up practice. |

All four rehearsals are **NOT YET EVIDENCED**. No fabricated dates or scores belong in the table. Run the four sessions on real dates before the actual assessment; repeat the specific failed behaviour rather than memorising a reassuring score.

## Assessment-day preparation

Confirm the room, permitted time, display resolution, audio needs, network and allowed backup format. Open only tabs without sensitive information. Prepare the live app, local fallback, copied fixtures and authentic screenshots; close email, database settings and token-bearing links. Keep the full release SHA and metric denominators on the backup cue sheet. Check the camera on the actual demonstration device beforehand; upload is the fallback. This is preparation, not an instruction to expand product scope.
