# Official presentation rubric matrix

Authority: supplied **CSC1250 Proposal Marking Scheme — Presentation**, pages 1–2. Four criteria total **40**; Excellent is **9–10**. This is a preparation score for the written slide plan, notes, demonstration and defence pack. It does not award or predict an observed live mark. No actual rehearsal, audience assessment or oral defence record was supplied.

| Criterion | Excellent requirement | Prepared evidence | User-dependent requirement | Remaining risk | Action | Expected mark |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Relevance of proposed idea | Highly relevant; addresses key issues and demonstrates significant potential impact. | 30/60-second openings; slides 1–3; defined unfamiliar-content decision; verified three-system gap and bounded workflow contribution. | Explain the decision and why a common explanation/private history matters in the first minute. | Potential value is logically argued, but no stakeholder need/comprehension study demonstrates its magnitude; significance remains examiner judgement. | Rehearse a concrete authored scenario and state potential value without claiming measured prevention or unique functionality. | **9/10 preparation**: plausible potential impact is supported by design/research, but its magnitude and audience reception are unmeasured. |
| Knowledge | Deep understanding; intelligent responses. | 68 questions, each with short/deeper answer, exact technical evidence and common mistake; slides cover architecture, methods, security, metrics and limits. | Explain each answer in your own words, navigate evidence and respond to follow-up challenges. | A prepared answer bank cannot establish the presenter's actual understanding or ability to answer an unfamiliar follow-up. | Complete technical accuracy and hostile Q&A rehearsals; record missed concepts and practise them again. | **9/10 preparation**: comprehensive material is present, but no assessed mock defence establishes that the training has succeeded. |
| Flow and organisation | Smooth, organised presentation with in-depth elaboration. | 13-slide narrative; per-slide purpose/visual/text ceiling/notes/transition/time; 795 seconds including 150-second demo; failure fallback. | Deliver transitions, explain diagrams and results, and adapt to the official time allocation. | Timing is allocated rather than measured; slide-reading, network delay or overlong Q&A can disrupt the story. | Confirm the time limit; run the timed rehearsal with real clicks and offline fallback; refine your spoken elaboration without changing methods. | **9/10 preparation**: a complete coherent sequence exists; smooth timed execution is not yet evidenced. |
| Delivery skills | Clear speech, audible to the whole audience, excellent eye contact. | Voice/pace/pauses/projection, 70–80% audience-focus practice target, posture, explain-before-click, direct-answer framework and four structured rehearsals. | Speak audibly, make natural eye contact and respond confidently without reading. | No room/audience observation, audio record or no-notes performance demonstrates delivery ability. | Record and review all four rehearsals; ask a person at the back to check audibility and a reviewer to track reading/fillers. | **8/10 preparation**: practical training is ready, but the delivery-feedback loop has not been executed. |

The remaining reasons an examiner could refuse 40/40 are weak articulation of significance, memorised answers without understanding, confusion between ML and deterministic methods, misstated metrics/CRC/privacy, timing or demonstration failures, reading slides, low projection, limited eye contact and unmeasured live performance.
