# Conclusion

SCAMGUARD demonstrates a working integration of Message, URL, Phone and QR assessment with private account and history workflows. Its technical contribution is the orchestration of reproducible local learned components, explicit deterministic evidence, bounded QR input handling and session-derived ownership. A common explanation structure preserves the distinctions between risk, confidence and insufficient evidence instead of claiming a single comparable fraud probability.

The proposal objectives are identify, design, develop and evaluate. Requirements and design evidence support O1–O2, and the four implemented modes and private workspace support O3. Functional tests and frozen classifier evaluation support the technical portions of O4. Message macro F1 is 0.895235 and URL macro F1 is 0.984698 on their documented held-out datasets, exceeding the proposed 0.85 target within those datasets. O4 remains partially complete because the proposed volunteer usability study has not been conducted. Current technical and production evidence is recorded in FINAL_COMPLETION_AUDIT_2026-10-20.md; September totals remain historical.

Those results establish selected technical behaviours rather than general scam-prevention effectiveness. Historical and collection-biased datasets constrain the learned components. Phone metadata cannot identify a caller; QR decoding and payment CRC cannot establish a legitimate recipient. Automated browser streams do not replace physical-device records, and informal owner acceptance does not constitute a usability study. Production readiness does not prove continuous availability or successful inbox delivery.

The project therefore fulfils its implemented decision-support scope while retaining explicit limits on the claims it can support. The most valuable further validation is external model testing, user comprehension and accessibility work, physical-device checks and recovery verification. These activities should follow the documented evidence protocols rather than be represented as completed results. References and traceability allow an examiner to inspect both the contribution and its limits.
